What's new in AnatomyGo

AnatomyGo is a free 3D human anatomy atlas for medical, physiotherapy, nursing and dental students. It runs in any web browser on a phone or computer, with no sign-up, and as an Android app. It has 2,234 modelled structures from BodyParts3D, and 773 pins that mark 750 bone landmarks and muscle attachments on the 3D model, including the origins and insertions of 14 muscles.

  • 549 new pins, so 7 in 10 of all pins are new this week.
  • Bones: the bones students open most now have their landmarks pinned and described: hip bone, femur, tibia, humerus, radius, ulna, scapula, clavicle, sternum, sacrum, atlas, axis, a lumbar vertebra, the mandible and the skull bones (frontal, parietal, temporal, occipital, sphenoid, ethmoid, zygomatic, maxilla and nasal).

Questions students ask

Is AnatomyGo free?

Yes. The website is free with no account, and the Android app is free on Google Play.

Does it work on a phone?

Yes. It runs in any modern browser with WebGL on phones, tablets and computers, and as an Android app.

Where can I see muscle origins and insertions in 3D?

Open a muscle in AnatomyGo and isolate it: its origins show in red and its insertions in blue, next to the bone landmarks they attach to. The muscles covered so far are listed above.

Where does the 3D model come from?

From BodyParts3D by the Database Center for Life Science (Japan), licensed CC BY-SA 2.1 Japan. It has 2,234 structures across 15 body systems.

How accurate are the pins?

Most pins were placed by measuring the 3D model and checked against published anatomy references. Every pin stays open to community review, and pins that have not been reviewed yet are marked as such. If you spot a wrong pin, say so in the Playground.

Can I link to one structure?

Yes. A link like anatomygo.in/?structure=FJ1433&isolate=1 opens the atlas on that structure with its pins.
